Terms of Use

The rules for using Yappr, in plain words

Devnet Notice

This release of Yappr runs on a Dash Platform devnet. Credits and test DASH have no monetary value. The network can be reset, and Yappr's mainnet launch may start fresh, so don't count on anything you post here being kept.

About these terms

These terms cover the Yappr app at yap.pr and the Yappr mobile apps. By signing in or posting, you agree to these terms and the Community Rules below. The Privacy Policy explains what is public and what is not.

What Yappr is

Yappr is open-source software for interacting with Dash Platform, a decentralized public network. Your posts, likes, follows and profile are records on that network. Yappr has no centralized moderation. Its authors cannot change, remove or recover your data on Dash Platform.

Other apps can read and display the same records. These terms cover Yappr; other apps have their own terms.

Who can use Yappr

You must be at least 18 years old, and able to agree to these terms where you live. Don’t use Yappr if the law, or a ban from the community-elected moderators, says you can’t.

Your identity and keys

  • Your keys are your account. You sign in with your Dash identity's private key. Yappr’s authors cannot reset or recover your keys if you lose them.
  • Keep them safe. Anyone with your keys can post as you and spend your credits. Sign out on devices you share.
  • You are responsible for what you do with them. Everything signed with your keys is attributed to your identity.

Your content

You own what you post. Because Dash Platform is public, anyone, using Yappr or another app, can read, display, share and quote it. By posting, you agree to that.

Treat everything you post as public and permanent. You can delete your posts and most other actions, but deleting does not erase them from the network's history or from people who already saw or saved them. The Privacy Policy explains what deleting does.

Community Rules

Follow these rules when using Yappr: for posts, replies, profiles, messages, blogs, stores, items and reviews. Community-elected teams moderate on-chain content under their published charters.

  • Nothing illegal. Don’t post, sell or link to anything that is illegal where you or the people you share it with live.
  • No sexual content involving minors. Do not post, share or link to sexual content involving minors. If a child is in danger, contact your local police.
  • No harassment or bullying. Don’t target, insult, intimidate or pile on other people, and don’t publish someone’s private information.
  • No hate. Don’t attack people for who they are, such as their race, ethnicity, religion, gender, sexuality or disability.
  • No violence or threats. Don’t threaten, incite or glorify violence.
  • No promotion of self-harm. Don’t encourage suicide or self-injury.
  • No spam or scams. No repetitive or misleading content, fake giveaways, phishing or fraud.
  • No impersonation. Don’t pretend to be another person or organisation.
  • Mark sensitive content. Mark posts with nudity, gore or other graphic content as sensitive, so Yappr hides them until each viewer chooses to see them.
  • Respect other people’s rights. Only post what you have the right to share, including other people’s photos, writing and trademarks.

Breaking them can get your content removed and your account warned, suspended or banned.

Moderation

The social, blog and storefront contracts each have an on-chain moderation team, elected by the community through masternode votes. These teams are independent of Yappr. Each team publishes a charter, and its removals, warnings, suspensions and bans must cite a reason from that charter. Within their contracts, moderators can:

  • remove posts, replies and the Yappr parts of a profile (such as the banner, website and links);
  • remove blogs, blog posts and comments;
  • remove stores, items and reviews;
  • warn, suspend or ban an account from the part of Yappr they moderate.

Moderation happens in the open. Every removal, warning, suspension or ban leaves a public record of who did it, to what, and when.

Before a team is elected, posting and reporting may be closed under the contract’s rules. Yappr’s authors do not handle reports or direct the elected teams.

Reporting

Use Report, where available, to publish an on-chain report for the community-elected moderators. Reports are public: the person you report can see that you reported them, the reason you chose and anything you wrote.

General questions: support@dash.org. Email does not file an on-chain report or guarantee action by moderators. Yappr’s authors cannot take down content or recover an account, funds or data. If a child is in danger, contact your local police first.

Stores and payments

  • Yappr is not a party to any sale. Stores are run by their owners. A purchase is an agreement between you and the seller.
  • You pay the seller directly, from your own wallet to the address the seller lists. Yappr never holds the money, and there is no escrow, refund or chargeback through Yappr. Sort out problems with the seller.
  • Sellers are responsible for what they sell, for delivering it, and for following the law, including tax, consumer protection, and shipping rules.
  • Reviews are public and tied to an order. Store moderators can remove stores, items and reviews that break the Community Rules.
  • On devnet, test DASH has no value, but a seller can list real payment addresses, including mainnet Dash and other cryptocurrencies. Check before you send anything of value.

Tips go straight to the person: credits or YAPP, where offered, move from your identity to theirs, and other cryptocurrencies go from your own wallet to the address they list. Yappr never holds them.

Costs

Writing to Dash Platform costs credits, paid from your identity's balance. Some actions also carry a small moderation fee that goes to the elected moderators. Posting, replying and liking can be paid in YAPP, Yappr’s in-app token. New accounts get a starter amount. While you pay in YAPP, the network fee is usually covered for you. Once your YAPP runs out, or if you choose, you pay in credits. YAPP has no monetary value, and Yappr never sells it. YAPP can’t be sent to other people. On devnet, credits are free.

Because it runs on a public network that nobody fully controls, mistakes can't always be undone and data can't always be recovered. To the extent the law allows, Yappr’s authors and contributors are not liable for any loss that comes from using Yappr, including lost keys, lost funds, or content posted by others.
