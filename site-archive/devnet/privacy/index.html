Privacy Policy

What is public on Yappr, what is private, and who sees what

Devnet Notice

This release of Yappr runs on a Dash Platform devnet. The privacy rules below apply in full, so don't post anything you wouldn't want public.

The short version

  • Almost everything you do on Yappr is public and permanent, because it is written to Dash Platform.
  • Messages, private-feed posts, what you order and shipping addresses are encrypted, but some facts about them are still visible.
  • Yappr has no accounts database or ads, and does not sell your data. Your device reads and writes records directly to Dash Platform.
  • Your keys stay on your device, or, if you add a passkey, on the network encrypted so only that passkey can open them. Yappr’s authors never receive them.
  • The website uses Cloudflare Web Analytics to count visits. It sets no cookies.

Public by design

Yappr writes to Dash Platform, a public ledger that anyone can read with Yappr or any other tool. Everything below is public and linked to your identity:

  • Your username, identity ID and credit balance
  • Your profile: name, bio, pronouns, avatar, banner, website, location, links and payment addresses
  • Your posts, replies and quotes, and the media they link to
  • Who you follow, who follows you, and follow requests to private feeds
  • Likes, bookmarks and poll votes
  • Who you block, any note you add to a block, and whose block lists you follow
  • Reports you file: what you reported, why, and anything you wrote
  • Who you approve to read your private feed
  • Your blogs, blog posts and comments, and the blogs you follow
  • Your store and items, the reviews you write, and that you ordered from a store and when
  • Standing for a moderation team, and votes in its election
  • What moderators do to your account or content, and the reasons they give
  • If you add passkeys: how many you have and when you added them, and your sign-in keys, encrypted so only that passkey can open them
  • The time of every action

What is encrypted, and its limits

Direct messages

Message content, and who you are talking to, are hidden from people reading the network. Some things still show:

  • that you send messages, how many, when, and roughly how long they are;
  • the network nodes your app asks for new messages can, over time, work out who you talk to and which groups you are in;
  • if two people often write moments apart, someone watching closely could guess they are talking;
  • everyone in a conversation or group can read it, and keeps their copy; group members see messages from when they joined;
  • anyone who gets your encryption key can read your whole message history. Keep it as safe as your sign-in key.

Private feeds

Private posts can only be read by the followers you approve. That you posted, when, who you approved, and who asked to follow are public. Followers you approve can also read your earlier private posts, unless you renewed your private feed with a fresh start. Followers you remove keep whatever they could already read.

Images are not encrypted. A private post's text and the link to its image are encrypted, but the image file itself is uploaded as is. The upload service, IPFS gateways, and anyone who finds its address can see it, so don't attach anything you need to keep private.

Reports open private posts to moderators. If someone reports one of your private posts, the report gives the moderators the key to read it. That key also opens the earlier posts in the same private feed.

Orders and addresses

What you ordered and your shipping address are encrypted so only you and the seller can read them. That you placed an order with a store, when, and its status are public. Tracking numbers, carrier details and seller notes are encrypted for you and the seller. Saved addresses are encrypted so only you can read them.

Reports

A report is public. The person you report can see that you reported them, the reason you chose and anything you wrote. How the community-elected moderators resolved it is public too. Reports expire after 90 days. The teams are elected by the community through masternode votes and are independent of Yappr. Emailing support does not file an on-chain report.

Deleting

You can delete your posts, replies, likes, follows, bookmarks, blocks and more. A deleted item disappears from Yappr and from the network's current data, though a deleted post may leave a marker behind showing when it was posted and its hashtags. It does not disappear from the network's history, or from anyone who already saw, saved or copied it.

When moderators remove something, a few details (such as when it was posted and its hashtags) stay, along with a public record of the removal. Your Dash identity itself can't be deleted.

On your device

Yappr keeps your keys, settings, drafts and caches in your browser so it can work without a server. Signing out removes your keys. The Cookies & Storage page lists exactly what is stored and for how long. The Yappr mobile apps keep keys in the phone's secure storage.

Services your device talks to

Yappr has no servers of its own. To work, your browser or app connects to these services:

Dash Platform nodes
Every read and write goes to the network’s nodes. The nodes that answer you see your IP address and what you ask for.
GitHub Pages and Cloudflare
yap.pr is hosted on GitHub Pages and delivered through Cloudflare (some copies of the site run on Cloudflare Pages). Like any web host, they see your IP address and the pages you load.
Cloudflare Web Analytics
Counts page views and measures how fast pages load. It sets no cookies and builds no profile of you; it records the page address, the referring site, your browser and device type, and your country.
wsrv.nl
Images from other websites load through this public image proxy, so the site hosting an image is never contacted by your browser; if the proxy is down, a placeholder shows instead. wsrv.nl sees the image address and your IP address.
IPFS gateways
Images and files stored on IPFS load from public IPFS gateways (gateway.pinata.cloud, ipfs.4everland.io, ipfs.io, ipfs.dweb.link). They see your IP address and what you load.
Storacha and Pinata
Only if you connect your own account to upload media. Uploads go from your browser to that service, under its terms.
YouTube
For YouTube links, the thumbnail loads from YouTube. The video player loads from youtube-nocookie.com only when you press play.
Link previews
Most link previews are made from the link itself, without contacting the website. Links to IPFS and a few image hosts (such as Imgur, Reddit, Giphy, GitHub and X) are read directly from those services. You can turn previews off in Settings › Privacy.
Dash block explorer (Insight)
When you pay in DASH, the app watches the payment address to confirm your payment arrived. The explorer sees your IP address and that address.
CoinGecko and CryptoCompare
When a payment code shows an approximate value in your currency, the price comes from these services.
Google Fonts
Blogs that use a custom font load it from Google Fonts, which sees your IP address.
Getting the Dash wallet
The sign-in screen links to dash.org, where you can download the Dash wallet.

Contact

Questions about privacy: support@dash.org. Yappr is open-source software for Dash Platform, a decentralized system. Its authors cannot change, remove or recover data on that network, and do not handle moderation requests.
